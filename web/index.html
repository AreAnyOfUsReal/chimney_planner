These answers are an example: a one-story ranch with a wood stove. Replace them with your own measurements.
How to measure
  • Heights start at the floor the stove or fireplace stands on.
  • Positions are at the chimney's center: the middle of where the hole will be.
  • One exception: the distance to the ridge is measured from the chimney's uphill (higher) side, because that is where the roof is closest to the top of the chimney.

Does the chimney go straight up?

An offset is a pair of elbows that shifts the chimney sideways to clear a joist, rafter or ridge beam. DuraTech allows up to 2. The shifts listed are the ones Duravent's elbow chart makes, so pick the one at or just above the shift you need; a smaller shift than the chart's smallest can't be built.

Height available is the vertical room where the jog happens, for example from the top of the ceiling joists to the underside of the rafters. With Auto, the planner uses 15° elbows if they fit and 30° if not. The line under each offset shows the shift, the height it takes and what's left over.

3 Attic Information

The attic is where the chimney gets its attic insulation shield and where an offset usually goes, so the planner needs to know how much open space is up there. Measure in the attic, directly above where the chimney's center will be.
